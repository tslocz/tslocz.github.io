About me

I am an associate professor in the Department of Economics at Brandeis University. I am also an affiliate at CESifo and a research fellow at the IZA@LISER Network. My research interests are in econometrics and applied microeconomics, with a particular focus on causal inference.

You can find further information about my research and other activities on this website. You are also welcome to visit my profiles at Google Scholar, IDEAS, and IZA@LISER.

A rough guide to pronouncing my last name is “Swatch-in-ski.”

Recent highlights

In January 2027, I will give a lecture on “Recent Developments in IV Methods with Heterogeneous Treatment Effects” in the AEA Recent Developments Series at the ASSA Annual Meeting in Washington, DC.

In October 2026, my paper “A Practical Guide to Instrumental Variables Methods with Heterogeneous Treatment Effects” (with Liyang Sun and S. Derya Uysal) was conditionally accepted for publication in the Journal of Economic Perspectives.

In October 2026, I presented my paper “Quantifying the Internal Validity of Weighted Estimands” (with Alexandre Poirier) at the Harvard/MIT Seminar in Econometrics and the Harvard Applied Statistics Workshop.

In November 2025, my paper “When Should We (Not) Interpret Linear IV Estimands as LATE?” was accepted for publication in the Review of Economic Studies.
